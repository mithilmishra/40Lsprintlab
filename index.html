Technique guide: handling, 0 ARR, finesse, stacking, reading ahead
1

Set up your handling

Handling decides how fast a piece can physically move. It costs nothing to change and usually gives the quickest drop in time. Find it in TETR.IO under Config → Handling, then copy the same numbers into this site's Settings so the drills feel the same.

SettingSuggested
ARR0 frames. The piece reaches the wall the moment DAS charges. See step 2.
SDFmax (∞). Soft drop becomes instant, so you never wait on it.
DCD0–1 frames. Go to 1 if a key you're still holding keeps throwing the next piece to the wall.
DASLower it 1 frame at a time. When quick taps start sliding all the way to the wall, go back up 1.
180Bind rotate 180 to a comfortable key. It saves a press on every flipped placement.

DCD (DAS cut delay) pauses a held left or right for that many frames right after you rotate or a new piece spawns. Expect to be a little slower for a few days after any handling change.

2

Move to 0 ARR

It's faster. Every frame of ARR adds about 17 ms per column a piece slides after DAS charges. At 0 ARR it is at the wall immediately.

It's more consistent. At 0 ARR, holding a key always means "go to the wall". Every move is a tap or a full slide, so you never time a release or watch the piece travel, and finesse becomes exact.

  • Change it in TETR.IO and in this site's Settings on the same day, and don't switch back. Expect a slower week.
  • The rule: spots one or two columns from spawn are taps; anything nearer a wall is a hold to the wall, plus one tap back if needed. The Finesse chart lists every case.
  • Leave Flag partial slides on in the Trainer. It marks every slide you stop short of the wall, a habit that stops working at 0 ARR.
  • Let go of left or right before you hard drop, unless the next piece is going to the same wall.
3

Clean up your finesse

Finesse means reaching each spot with the fewest presses. Perfect finesse works out to about 3 key presses per piece including the hard drop (about 2.8 with 180). Every extra press adds up over 100 pieces.

  • Use the Trainer for 10 minutes a day with Focus weak spots on. It times every placement and keeps serving the ones you miss or place slowly until they're down pat.
  • Most faults come from tapping 3 times where a hold to the wall plus one tap does it in 2, or rotating twice instead of using 180.
  • Watch keys per piece and finesse faults on the TETR.IO results screen, and re-import your runs to see them trend.
4

Stack for speed

  • Keep the stack flat and low. A tall stack doesn't make you faster; it only makes a misdrop more expensive.
  • Don't wait for an I piece with a messy board. Take the double or triple and keep moving.
  • Skip spins and tucks. Soft drops and slides cost time and keys and buy nothing in 40L.
  • Hold with a purpose, such as saving an I or dodging an awkward S or Z. Every hold is a press and a decision.
  • From about line 30, only place pieces that complete lines, so you finish with an almost empty board.
5

Read ahead

Fast players rarely decide where a piece goes when it spawns; they decided while the last one was falling.

  • While you place the current piece, pick a spot for the next one in the queue.
  • Play Sprint here with the suggested spot on and never stop moving. The pause chart afterwards shows exactly where you stalled.
  • Speed up only once those runs feel smooth. Pauses usually cost more time than slow fingers.
